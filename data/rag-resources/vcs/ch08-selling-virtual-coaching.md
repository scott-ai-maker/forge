### Defining Sales

Chapter 8Selling Virtual CoachingLesson 1Introduction to Sales, Pricing, and PackagingDefining SalesPurchasing is a solution to a problem. When consumers have an issue or unmet need, they typically purchase a product or service they feel will solve that specific problem or meet that specific need. As soon as the transaction is complete, the company this product or service was purchased from has made a sale. Selling a product or service is problem solving. Consequently, sales are the process of truthfully and successfully conveying that a company’s product or service offering truly solves the consumer’s problem.
Fitness business entrepreneurs take on many different duties, especially when starting and growing their business. One of the most important tasks a virtual fitness business entrepreneur will have is that of a salesperson. As previously mentioned, without sales, a company will cease to exist. Many business owners, and people in general, have an aversion to sales. Salespeople are sometimes viewed as manipulative and willing to do whatever it takes to make a sale—even at the customer’s expense. The stereotypical salesperson shows little regard for the interest of the customer and cares more about getting money in their pocket. In contrast, the fitness business entrepreneur is not a stereotypical salesperson and will not be viewed as such, provided they have a long-term mindset about their business and emphasize putting their customers’ interests first. Fitness business entrepreneurs generally are passionate about the product or service they offer and believe it will make a significant impact on their client’s life. By putting ethics and clients’ interests first, there is no need to worry about coming across as a manipulative or unethical salesperson. While it probably does not need to be stated, manipulation and poor ethics do not produce results in the long run.
As many retail and financial transactions now take place via e-commerce web and mobile applications, prospective clients will also likely expect a virtual coaching business to conduct its sales online. Leveraging the familiarity that potential clients already have with e-commerce, automated sales processes can be developed by the fitness professional that will turn their business into a smooth-running sales machine.
Inbound and Outbound SalesSales can be divided into two categories: inbound sales and outbound sales. Outbound sales are sales initiated by the business—in other words, the business contacted the client first to attempt a sale. Outbound sales are associated with techniques such as cold calling, where the business contacts prospective clients unsolicited.
Antonov Maxim/Shutterstock.comConversely, inbound sales are sales initiated by the client—in other words, the client contacted the business looking to purchase a particular product or service.

The virtual fitness professional’s customer acquisition strategy should be built around an inbound sales approach. Inbound prospective clients include those who have found the business via content-driven platforms like social media channels, blogs, videos, podcasts, etc. Inbound strategies are centered on establishing trust and building rapport with prospective customers. Successful inbound methods help establish the brand as a trusted advisor through high-quality content. By earning prospective clients’ trust and interest they will be more receptive to sales tools the fitness professional entrepreneur will utilize to generate business, such as automated e-mail marketing. With that in mind, this chapter will focus primarily on inbound sales processes.

### Why People Make Purchases

Chapter 8Selling Virtual CoachingLesson 1Introduction to Sales, Pricing, and PackagingWhy People Make PurchasesConsumers purchase products or services to solve a problem. When clients and potential clients are not satisfied with their current health and fitness or are not attaining their desired fitness and health goals, they typically seek out goods and services to help fulfill that desire. For the virtual fitness professional, most of their clients will have similar goals, including weight loss, building muscle, obtaining better overall health, improving performance, etc. The fitness professional’s mission is to identify the problems their clients are facing, inform the client how their programs and services can help solve their problem, and ethically guide the future client in their purchase of the business’s services (Figure: Fitness Professional’s Mission).
FigureFitness Professional's MissionIdentify the ProblemUtilizing the target market identified in their business plan, the fitness professional has a well-defined market audience. Correctly identifying the specific problems clients are seeking to solve is key to developing a successful sales funnel. Note that a client’s problem or problems may change over time. It is always important to obtain feedback on how the goods and services are meeting the needs of the target market, not only from clients but also from potential clients who do not currently purchase the service.
When starting a business, the fitness professional does not always have enough, or any, feedback from their potential market. To solve this, the fitness professional must conduct market research to understand the most common challenges prospective clients are facing. At this stage, the fitness professional may not have clients or even potential clients whom they can contact for useful feedback, but they can still develop an idea based on their own knowledge and experience about what problems and challenges the market needs to solve and overcome. The focus of the initial sales strategy should be the problems that currently exist in the market that inspired the fitness professional to develop their own virtual fitness business.

### Solve the Problem

Chapter 8Selling Virtual CoachingLesson 1Introduction to Sales, Pricing, and PackagingSolve the ProblemWith the problem identified, and the fitness professional already having established that they can solve the problem with their service, the next step is to understand how to successfully communicate the problem and solution to a potential client. Advertising and marketing are the keys to conveying this message.
HstrongART/Shutterstock.comMarketing and sales are closely related, and advertisements are an integral part of sales. Advertisements typically convey solutions to identified problems. Consider television ads for soft drinks. They largely focus on people relaxing, quenching their thirst, and cooling off with an ice-cold drink. The problem is thirst and the solution is the soft drink. If an ad for a soft drink stated, “You’re going to be thirsty sometime, and when you are, buy our drink,” the advertisement would not have much impact. However, if the advertisement clearly demonstrates how the product can fix a problem, the impact is much greater. When attempting to convey that a product solves a problem, the fitness professional should be mindful to not only highlight the obvious solution but also focus on the reasons why the problem exists in the first place.

### Emotional Factors in Purchasing

Chapter 8Selling Virtual CoachingLesson 1Introduction to Sales, Pricing, and PackagingEmotional Factors in PurchasingConsumers buy products and services for a variety of reasons. The Consumer Emotional Intelligence Scale (Kidwell et al., 2008) was designed to investigate the role that emotional factors play in consumer’s purchasing decisions. The three emotional factors examined include self-concept connection, emotional attachment, and brand love. This study found focusing on emotional connections with consumers, rather than traditional features and benefits, resulted in greater brand loyalty. For the fitness entrepreneur, this should be the main focus of the sales strategy. When attempting to build an emotional connection with customers, authenticity is essential, with 81% of consumers stating they must be able to trust a brand (Edelman, 2019).
With a solid understanding of the problems their programs and services solve, the virtual fitness professional can put themselves in their clients’ and prospective clients’ shoes and imagine the emotions that they may feel about the problems they are experiencing. Selling based on emotions is not an attempt to take advantage of the negative emotions people feel due to a certain problem but to highlight the positive emotions a person will feel when they realize the fitness entrepreneur’s product solves the problem.

    What are two possible positive emotions for each subproblem once solved?

### Prices and Packages

Chapter 8Selling Virtual CoachingLesson 1Introduction to Sales, Pricing, and PackagingPrices and PackagesWhen pricing a core product, a fitness professional must ensure their pricing aligns with the target market being served. A very low-priced service being offered to high-income earners will have a difficult time finding clients who value the service. A service whose target market is seniors on a fixed income may not find clients if the service is priced too high. The pricing level of a product should not be confused with the quality or value of a product. A product can be economically priced but of high quality and value, and conversely, a luxury priced product can be of very poor quality and not offer much value.
Pricing StrategiesThe business plan should define the target market and provide a pricing analysis of competitors in the market. The virtual fitness professional should set the base price of the service in the range of market analysis. Once the base price of a product is set, the fitness professional can then use different pricing strategies to aid in the sales process. The four pricing strategies discussed in Chapter 2: Building a Business include economy, skimming, penetration, and premium pricing. Additionally, the fitness professional may also consider using promotional pricing and bundle pricing strategies (Figure: Pricing Strategies).

Penetration pricing is used to build interest in a new product. With this method, the standard price of the product is initially reduced as an introductory offering. However, this pricing strategy may result in a price so low the business cannot sustain selling at this rate for long periods of time. When penetration pricing is used, a clear objective, or cut-off, must be noted. Penetration pricing objectives may include initially obtaining a specific number of clients, converting those clients to higher-priced programs or services, increasing client referrals, or having a specific and publicly announced end date for the promotion.
FigurePricing StrategiesVectorpocket/Shutterstock.comPromotional pricing is similar to penetration pricing but with a few distinct differences. First, promotional pricing is offered to an existing product or service. Second, the discount should not be as low as a penetration pricing discount. With an existing and established product, offering a very large discount runs the risk of upsetting existing customers, along with devaluing the services that are provided. If a product or service is regularly on sale, most customers will wait until promotional pricing is offered again, rather than purchase at the standard price.

Bundle pricing is a strategy that offers discounts for buying in bulk. This can either be buying one product in bulk or purchasing multiple products together for a discounted price. For example, different bundle discounts could be offered on monthly coaching if a client pre-pays for 3, 6, or 12 months of coaching when compared to paying monthly. A bundle discount for clients who purchase multiple products would cover clients who purchased fitness training and nutrition coaching together instead of purchasing fitness training or nutrition coaching alone.

### Tracking Sales

Chapter 8Selling Virtual CoachingLesson 2Sales Goals and Process CustomizationTracking SalesWith sales goals established, the fitness professional must continually track their sales data to ensure realistic goals have been set. Using the metrics of daily, weekly, and monthly prospects, leads, and customers, the fitness entrepreneur has a powerful dataset for tracking sales and revenue. Each metric will have unique data points and places to source this data. To obtain sales data, the source of each metric being tracked must be clearly defined.
Each business should have personalized definitions of prospects, leads, and customers, but, in general, they will be similar to the following parameters for tracking. Prospects include unique views on the company website, comments on social media, podcast downloads, and video views. Prospects should be tracked daily. Leads include inbound e-mails and contact form submissions from the company website, direct messages on social media, inbound phone calls, downloading free ebooks in exchange for the lead’s email address, and receiving any contact information from other means. Lead data will be tracked daily but recorded in the sales tracker weekly. Customers include anyone who has made their first purchase from the company or a client who returns after previously ending their services. For sales goals tracking, this number generally does not include those on recurring subscriptions. Customer data will be tracked daily but only recorded in the sales goal tracker monthly.
Eviart/Shutterstock.comEach sales stage category should be updated weekly with the previous week’s data. This allows the fitness entrepreneur to track and stay on top of sales metrics and identify if they start to trend down, stay consistent, or trend upward. While nothing may need to be modified, it allows for visibility and tracking of revenue goals, and the fitness professional can determine how they may average out over time.
Helpful HintJust as declining sales are something the entrepreneur should be on alert for, explosive sales are also something to be aware of. Growth is vital for a business, but explosive growth is not sustainable for most small businesses and can have several negative consequences.

### Prospecting for New Clients

Chapter 8Selling Virtual CoachingLesson 2Sales Goals and Process CustomizationProspecting for New Clients Prospecting is the act of searching for new clients. Prospects turn into leads and leads turn into clients. Finding prospects can often be a challenging and intimidating aspect of sales for small business owners, typically with outbound sales or outbound prospecting. The method of cold-calling prospects from a purchased sales list, such as telemarketing, is typically no longer used. There are many ways to find prospects for leads and, ultimately, new clients. This section will focus on how to utilize the business’s target audience, as defined in the business plan, and marketing plan to automate the prospecting process.
FigureCommon Sales TypesThere are two types of sales: business to consumer sales (B2C) and business to business sales (B2B) (Figure: Common Sales Types). Fitness professionals who target the B2B market might be focused on services such as corporate wellness programs. In B2B marketing, the target customer is a business. Most virtual fitness professional’s sales will fall into the B2C category, in which the target customer is an individual consumer. This section will focus primarily on the B2C category.
Prospecting, just like sales and marketing, can be broken down into inbound prospecting and outbound prospecting. Inbound prospecting builds on the marketing processes developed and will allow the prospecting process to become largely automated. An inbound prospect finds the company’s content through various channels and content types defined in the marketing plan. These channels and content can include social media, search engines, pay-per-click (PPC) advertising, email marketing, videos, infographics, newsletters, etc. The goal is to convert the prospect who viewed or consumed the marketing content into a lead, and that lead in turn will provide the business with their contact information. This process will be discussed in greater detail later in this chapter. Outbound prospecting is when the target customer audience is first identified by the business and then, the business reaches out to the target customer and initiates contact. Outbound prospecting is an effective prospecting technique, but it is time-consuming and limits the ability to automate the prospecting process.
Prospecting must be continually performed, as businesses generally always need new customers. Prospecting can be developed and simplified into a repeatable and scalable process by focusing on three areas (Figure: Prospecting Process). First, clearly define the target market. Second, identify a clear prospecting strategy of how and where prospects will be found. Finally, establish a clear new prospecting strategy to ensure new prospects continue to become aware of the company.

### Prospecting Strategy

Chapter 8Selling Virtual CoachingLesson 2Sales Goals and Process CustomizationProspecting StrategyThe second step in automating the prospecting process is developing a strategy for how the business will find new prospects. An example outbound prospecting strategy would be to purchase a list of consumers who fit the target audience and then, either call or e-mail them. This is a time consuming and expensive process, which typically yields poor results.
Utilizing an inbound prospecting approach, the virtual fitness entrepreneur can take advantage of the marketing plan and capitalize on existing content to attract prospective clients. For example, utilizing blog posts that have followed a strategic search engine optimization (SEO) approach, as defined by the marketing strategy, will bring in consumers who are in the business’s target audience directly to the company’s website. These prospects are very high quality because they qualified themselves into the target audience by searching for a question the business can, and already has, answered in their blog post content.
Sales Strategy Questions for Prospecting StrategyThe fitness professional can define how the business will obtain new prospects by answering three questions.


    Based on the marketing strategy, what are the top three content types that will be utilized by the business to attract prospects (videos, infographics, blogs, podcasts, etc.)?
    For each of the content marketing types identified, what incentive will be created and offered for the consumer to share their contact information with the business and become a lead?
    For each incentive, how will the fitness business entrepreneur follow up with the newly created lead? How can this process be automated?

Coach's CornerThe prospecting strategy must focus on refining the content marketing strategy to ensure content developed is directed toward the target audience.

For example, if a fitness professional's target audience is older adults, they may write blog posts about protein intake for seniors. The prospecting strategy should ensure that the blog is optimized for commonly used terms the prospects will type into a search engine.

If the business is utilizing videos in their marketing, the prospecting strategy should ensure the videos offer an incentive for prospects to provide their contact information and become leads.
A well-rounded business needs to have prospects and, consequently, sales coming from more than one place. Having multiple sales pipelines will ensure the business has enough diversity and a strong foundation of prospects that can withstand market changes over time.

### Customizing the Sales Process

Chapter 8Selling Virtual CoachingLesson 2Sales Goals and Process CustomizationCustomizing the Sales Process With an automated prospecting process, the fitness professional can provide a highly customized sales process designed to appeal to different subsets of their target market. The process of subdividing target markets is called market segmentation. Segmenting a marketing or sales list will allow the fitness professional to deliver highly customized and personalized sales messaging directly to leads.
Email list segmentation can be implemented starting at the prospect level. Specific marketing content can be created for different segments of the target audience. For example, a blog post about resistance training for busy mothers will attract a different segment of the same target audience when compared to a blog post about endurance training for busy mothers. The target audience is the same, but the segments, those interested in resistance training versus those interested in endurance training, are different.

Segmenting the target audience can be automated for the sales process when the prospect becomes a lead by providing their email address in exchange for an offer. ### Educating Prospective Clients

Chapter 8Selling Virtual CoachingLesson 2Sales Goals and Process CustomizationEducating Prospective ClientsThe purpose of the marketing strategy is to educate prospective clients about the existence of the company and its programs and services. The sales process continues educating leads about the solutions the fitness professional provides to the problems they are facing. This education should be customized to a lead’s segment as much as possible. Every lead will prefer to be educated on the aspects of the programs or services that directly apply to their individual needs. Two education techniques include offering a unique selling proposition and value proposition.
Offering a Unique Selling PropositionA unique selling proposition (USP) is a specific benefit a product or service provides the target market that separates a business from its competitors. It is important to highlight the true uniqueness of the programs and services offered. Mentioning features of a business or its services is simply not enough. There was a time when responding to an e-mail within 24 hours or offering text messaging to a client was unique and rare. However, at a time when more online coaching businesses are appearing, these features have now become the standard. The fitness business entrepreneur needs a USP that only they are able to bring to their clients. While the USP can be the same for different lead segments, it should be positioned differently to address the unique concerns of a particular segment.
Providing ValueThe value proposition is simply the value of the programs and services that are offered. This can describe the price of the service, service quality, and value trade-off, which is the lead saving money by choosing the service offered instead of spending more money on another option. Value propositions should educate leads on the value of the programs or services and be utilized to support the USP.

### Customer Onboarding Process

Chapter 8Selling Virtual CoachingLesson 3The Onboarding ProcessCustomer Onboarding Process Developing a systematic process around the customer sales cycle or sales strategy allows for tracking, refinement, directional pivots, reproducibility/scale, and efficiency in client acquisition. Prior to becoming paid clients, consumers will enter different stages based on where they are in the sales process. As mentioned previously, these sales stages are prospects, leads, and clients.
Prospects are potential customers who are just finding out about the service. This could be by reading a blog, watching a video, or simply browsing the fitness professional’s website. Prospects are starting to show interest in the business’s services; however, they have shown no interest beyond that.
Leads are prospects who have shown a greater-than-normal interest in the business’s product or service. Typically, a lead has reached out to the fitness professional in some form and has provided some type of personal information to initiate interaction and conversation. This contact information can come from filling out a form on the fitness professional’s website, entering an e-mail address in exchange for a free ebook, or interacting with the fitness professional on social media.
Customers or clients are leads who have become paid customers of the business. Once a person becomes a client, they do not simply stay at this stage. Existing clients can also re-enter a sales funnel and become a lead for service or product upgrades and add-ons. Clients need to be nurtured as much, if not more, than prospects and leads to ensure their retention.

Please note: Both prospects and leads can become clients—they do not have to follow the specific order or progression outlined to become a client. The prospect, lead, and customer path is simply the most common progression from consumer to paid customer.

### From Client to Brand Champion

Chapter 8Selling Virtual CoachingLesson 3The Onboarding ProcessFrom Client to Brand ChampionFor many businesses, once a prospect becomes a client, the sales process is over. The customer journey process has achieved its goal and the business has a new client. However, for the fitness professional entrepreneur, the process has only just begun. Because it is easier and more cost-effective to sell to existing customers compared to acquiring new customers, the fitness professional should focus on maximizing a  customer's lifetime value (CLV). This can be done through delivering exceptional service, product add-ons, and upgrades. Upgrading a client who enrolled in online fitness training to online training plus nutrition coaching will result in a more profitable customer while also providing the customer with more tools to achieve their goals and overall success.
Food for ThoughtImagine that a client pays $200 per month for virtual fitness coaching. Assuming that the client has an average membership length of 4 months, the lifetime value of this client is $800. If this client upgrades to a package that includes virtual fitness and nutrition coaching at $250 per month, after their first month, the client has a lifetime value of $950. This is a 16% increase in revenue from the client’s original lifetime value. Over the course of a year, if 100 clients purchase the same upgrade, this would result in an extra $15,000 in revenue for the business.
Upgrading an existing client's services is yet another customer journey process. The previous customer journey examined turning a prospect into a client; however, turning an existing client into a brand champion client requires a different process. A champion is a client who has a higher than average CLV. The brand champion client is a champion of your business and your brand. They are a long-term client who purchases service upgrades and raves about the business to everyone they know. Not every client will become a brand champion, but the virtual fitness entrepreneur must have processes in place to be able to take clients to the champion level.
Customer lifetime value (CLV)The amount that a customer will spend with business over a lifetime.

### Sales Process

Chapter 8Selling Virtual CoachingLesson 3The Onboarding ProcessSales ProcessThe sales process is what happens after a prospect becomes a lead. The sales process is the journey that leads take to become paying clients. During the previous phase of prospecting for new clients, the consumer turned into a lead. At that point, the marketing process ended, and the sale process began.
The lead-to-client sales process is one that will illustrate to qualified leads that the fitness professional understands their needs, help build rapport, and show the potential client their concerns and desires are being heard. This allows the fitness professional to demonstrate their expertise in the area the lead is struggling with. This process is commonly referred to as lead nurturing. In an automated sales process, lead nurturing can easily be done via e-mail marketing.
With e-mail marketing automation, the fitness professional has the equivalent of highly personalized ads sent directly to their target audience who has already shown specific interest in their products or services. E-mail automation provides the fitness professional with an opportunity to perform market segmentation and provide highly personalized e-mails. E-mail automation should always be as segmented as possible to ensure the most personalized messaging possible is being delivered to the lead.
Eny Setiyowati/Shutterstock.comNot everyone on a fitness professional’s e-mail marketing list is a lead. When someone is first added to an e-mail list, they are a lead. This means the marketing strategy used by the fitness professional worked and a prospect was turned into a lead. The prospect showed interest, accepted an offer, and opted-in to an e-mail list and became a lead. The lead is then placed into e-mail automation software and enters into a specific e-mail automation workflow for new leads. This workflow is designed to provide e-mails that will build rapport and increase trust with leads. This is typically done over several days or weeks. By the time the workflow is complete, one of three things will happen:

    The lead becomes a client. The workflow was successful. Once the lead becomes a client, the new lead workflow ends immediately.
    The lead unsubscribes from the workflow. This can happen for any number of reasons. Please note: If the unsubscribe rate is more than .3% on the initial e-mail, then the content of the e-mails in the workflow should be assessed/re-examined for relevancy. Any of the following can influence open and unsubscribe rates and should be examined: the subject line copy, e-mail body copy, frequency or quantity of e-mails, day and/or time sent, and content design reassessed.
    The workflow ends and the lead does not become a client. The sales process did not work and the lead did not initiate a purchase.

In the outcome where the lead did not become a client, they are no longer a lead but are once again a prospect. They will still receive general marketing e-mails as part of the marketing strategy; however, they would no longer receive personalized lead nurturing e-mails. At this point, the prospect will need to be remarketed to until they are placed into another sales funnel or workflow.

### Building Rapport

Chapter 8Selling Virtual CoachingLesson 3The Onboarding ProcessBuilding Rapport Building rapport with consumers is not an easy task. If payment is collected too late, a client may be receiving free coaching, even if they do not intend to continue. Having consistent, automated billing also allows for the fitness professional to have predictable and consistent revenue coming into the business. Allowing a client to enter their own billing information into a website and subscribe to coaching via an online e-commerce platform integrated with the business’s merchant processor will allow payments and subscription fees to be automated. Sending out individual invoices to clients every month is time consuming and allows for potential errors in amounts billed and consistency in payments. Automated billing and payment processing take stress away from the fitness professional, allowing them to focus on more impactful activities such as content development, programming, and coaching.
