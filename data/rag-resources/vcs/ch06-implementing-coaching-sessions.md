### Session Preparation

Chapter 6Implementing Coaching SessionsLesson 1Session PreparationSession PreparationOperating in the virtual fitness space allows a fitness professional to interact with more clients than they would have been able to while doing in-person training. The time gaps between appointments are typically much smaller than in a typical face-to-face environment, and the fitness professional or nutrition coach can remain in the same location from session to session. Clients also benefit from the convenience of virtual training as they can access their coach from the comfort of their own homes and even on the go. Due to this increased accessibility, it is vital that the fitness professional be well-prepared and organized in their virtual training business. The quality of the fitness professional’s preparation may dictate the success of the session. Selecting appropriate video conferencing tools, scheduling and confirming appointments, and preparing workouts are the foundational requirements of a virtual training business.

Using applications that provide large storage space (e.g., Vimeo, Dropbox, or YouTube) is recommended for professionals sharing prerecorded workouts with their clients. For live, 1-to-1 or group virtual coaching sessions, video conferencing applications are recommended (e.g., Zoom, Skype, WebEx, Google Meets, etc.). Advancements in video conferencing technologies have made them considerably useful, essentially indispensable, tools for fitness professionals operating virtually. ### Confirming Appointments

Chapter 6Implementing Coaching SessionsLesson 1Session PreparationConfirming AppointmentsOnce a scheduling system is chosen, the fitness professional will need to determine which appointment confirmation system will best complement their business. Confirming appointments maximizes a fitness professional’s time, minimizes missed appointments, and provides appointment reminders to clients. Using a virtual scheduling tool is an efficient method for managing appointments as these tools can be programmed to provide reminders at specific times prior to an appointment. An efficient confirmation system should notify a client about an upcoming appointment 12 to 24 hours prior to its start. This time frame provides the fitness professional with enough notice to adjust their schedule and the client with enough notice to reschedule without penalty. If the fitness professional chooses not to use a scheduling tool it is imperative that they keep track of upcoming appointments and confirm them with their clients via e-mail, text message, or phone call.
Rawpixel.com/Shutterstock.comThe scheduling and confirming of appointments organizes and optimizes a virtual training business, but without accountability structures such as policies and signed agreements, issues of adherence may still come up. Designing and communicating a cancellation policy may aid in keeping clients and professionals accountable for their scheduled appointments.
A cancellation policy is a statement of the course of action to occur if an agreed-on meeting is not upheld in a predetermined manner or time frame. Cancellation policies are put in place to hold the client accountable to the agreed-on times of confirmed appointments. Additionally, a cancellation policy should allow the fitness professional enough time to fill the session should a client cancel. The penalty for failing to appear for a confirmed appointment could be as severe as a complete loss of a paid session, as mild as a small penalty fee, or a verbal warning. The cancellation policy must be determined by the fitness professional and should be clearly communicated to the client prior to engaging in the virtual training program. Cancellation policies are not intended to punish clients for mishaps. Rather, they are designed to encourage schedule integrity by providing appointment confirmation time constraints (e.g., 12 to 24 hours prior to the session or a 10-minute grace period). These confirmations not only hold participants accountable; they also act as helpful reminders and allow penalty-free margins for rescheduling.
Cancellation Policy ExamplesExample Cancellation Policies

    A client can cancel or reschedule an appointment 12 hours prior to the session, penalty-free.
    A client can cancel or reschedule an appointment 24 hours prior to a session, penalty-free, and is allotted two no-shows prior to penalization.
    A client who fails to appear for a confirmed appointment will be penalized 100% of their session cost.
    A client who fails to appear for a confirmed appointment will be penalized 50% of their session cost.
    A client is allotted two no-shows prior to penalization. Once the client exceeds two no-shows, they will be penalized 100% of their session cost for any further missed appointments.
    A client is granted a 15-minute grace period at the beginning of each session. This means a client has a 15-minute window to appear for a session before being penalized.

### Equipment Set-Up

Chapter 6Implementing Coaching SessionsLesson 1Session PreparationEquipment Set-UpFailing to check and prepare vital equipment prior to an appointment can drastically affect a virtual training business. If the fitness professional has a full week of scheduled appointments and their computer crashes, their audio or video system fails, or there is a loss of Internet connection, they will not only have a host of frustrated clients, but they may also lose future business. Fitness professionals should create an equipment checklist and go through the list at least 1 hour prior to the first appointment of each training day. Incorporating a daily equipment check routine will help mitigate any sudden or unexpected equipment breakdowns, allow time to rectify any issues that arise, and, if necessary, provide notice to scheduled clients.
wavebreakmedia/Shutterstock.comOnce the fitness professional has completed their equipment check, they will need to set up all equipment required for their upcoming virtual coaching sessions. This includes but is not limited to computer and recording systems being fully charged and in place, having appropriate lighting set up, ensuring all required training equipment is accessible, and that training programs are completed and accessible. It is also best practice for the fitness professional to perform an end-of-day checklist to account for any equipment that will need to be fully charged, cleaned, or turned off after each day of appointments.
Equipment Preparation Checklist❏  Camera and audio recording system functioning properly

❏  Computer connected and functioning as expected

❏  All battery-powered peripherals fully charged

❏  Lighting systems on and functioning

❏  All required training equipment accessible

❏  Client workout program(s) complete and ready to access

❏  Electronic equipment that does not require a charge (e.g. external lighting) unplugged from the power source until the next day of appointments

❏ Training equipment and environment cleaned and arranged in an appropriate location for the next day of appointments

### Program and Session Preparation

Chapter 6Implementing Coaching SessionsLesson 1Session PreparationProgram and Session PreparationTo maintain a successful virtual training business, the fitness professional should be prepared prior to the start of each and every session. A training session that is void of a predetermined training program or structure can lead to a multitude of complications. The fitness professional might forget what the client’s workout is on a particular day or accidentally confuse one client's program for another’s. By completing the design and planning of each client’s program and having it accessible prior to their session, the fitness professional can mitigate the likelihood of these complications. The saying, “failing to plan is planning to fail,” is a good reminder for fitness professionals engaging with clients in the virtual environment. Preparing training programs prior to a session provides a foundation and structure from which the fitness professional can make intelligent and effective alterations when necessary (Figure: Preparation).
FigurePreparationOnce the information is gathered from initial assessments, such as health and injury history, client limitations and preference, and physical assessments, the fitness professional will construct a training program for their client based on their individual goals. The fitness professional can utilize a variety of online training program builders, such as NASM EDGE, or choose to use something as simple as a spreadsheet program (e.g., Microsoft Excel, Google Sheets, etc.) or even pen and paper. Once developed, the training programs should be organized in such a way that they will be easy to track, record, and adjust when necessary.
Africa Studio/Shutterstock.comRegardless of which program development tool the fitness professional chooses to use, it is vital they have easy access to the programs prior to each virtual training session. Having training programs completed and accessible prior to appointments will mitigate complications and ensure program integrity during training sessions. As mentioned previously, a client’s program may need to be adjusted or altered at times, but having everything prepared and accessible will help the fitness professional make the most effective adjustments.

### Training Environment

Chapter 6Implementing Coaching SessionsLesson 2Training EnvironmentTraining EnvironmentBAZA Production/Shutterstock.comTo effectively implement virtual training sessions, the fitness professional will need an appropriate amount of space and a suitable environment. Virtual training clients watch and listen to movements and exercise cues as their only source of coaching. So, having adequate space and the environment to perform and instruct exercise movements is paramount. The client does not want to see their trainer struggling to direct the session due to a lack of organization or space.
Critical!Fitness professionals engaging in virtual training should make the following training environment considerations:


    Does the space have enough room to not only demonstrate exercises but also include enough space for the filming system to capture the entire body and movement demonstration in the frame?
    What camera angle is most suitable for the training session or exercise selection?
    What external sounds could the space be exposed to? Is the appropriate equipment available to mitigate any potential sound interruptions?
    What kind of lighting (fluorescent, skylight, natural light, etc.) does the space have? Is there a skylight or natural light? Will the time of day a session is conducted be a factor? Will the fitness professional need extra lighting equipment?
    What kind of flooring does the space have? Carpet? Hardwood? Tile? Gym flooring? Will the flooring be a hindrance to the style of the training session?
    Does the space have enough room for all the necessary training equipment?

SpacingThe fitness professional should first consider what style of movements they will be consistently demonstrating in their sessions. Perhaps the movements will be primarily dynamic, high-intensity exercises like plyometrics, or maybe the exercises will consist mostly of easy, restorative movements that are performed in a single location. Once the fitness professional has determined the training style to use, they should perform a practice run of a few of the primary exercises. The practice run is intended to ensure there is ample space for safe demonstration of the exercises, void of obstacles. If space limitations do exist, the fitness professional will need to make alterations to either the movement and exercise selection or the training space itself (e.g., not jumping as high or moving a coffee table).

### Filming

Chapter 6Implementing Coaching SessionsLesson 2Training EnvironmentFilmingOnce the practice run is complete and the training space is adjusted to complete all movement, the fitness professional should film some of the primary exercises and ensure that the chosen camera or filming system is able to capture each movement in its entirety. Optimal placement for a filming system is 6 to 8 feet from the camera. Depending on the filming method (phone, computer, external camera), this distance may have to be adjusted.

Another vital aspect of camera placement is the camera angle. The camera angle must provide the most optimal viewing experience to the client. Determining which perspective will allow the viewer to see the most important aspects of exercise form is an effective way to choose a camera angle. The camera angle not only shapes a client’s viewing experience but may even mold client perceptions of their instructor. Camera angles have even been suggested to affect viewer perception of trustworthiness.

### Sound and Audio

Chapter 6Implementing Coaching SessionsLesson 2Training EnvironmentSound and AudioScarc/Shutterstock.comAdministering virtual training is highly reliant on verbal cues and coaching. The fitness professional will not physically be with the client to spot them or make form adjustments. So, the client’s ability to clearly hear and understand the coach becomes vital. The fitness professional should consider investing in a shotgun or lavalier mic to achieve quality audio. While setting up the training environment, the fitness professional should assess potential external sounds that might disrupt a training session. If the training space is in an apartment, adjacent to a noisy freeway, or exposed to children or pets, precautions need to be taken to mitigate the likelihood of those sounds impacting the training session.

Soundproofing supplies such as foam soundproofing panels, blackout or noise reduction curtains, and carpets/rugs are cost-effective methods for absorbing unwanted external noise vibrations.
Investing in soundproofing materials can transform an unsuitable training environment into a calm, quiet space perfect for virtual training implementation. If the fitness professional has children, pets, or roommates, they should make necessary arrangements and schedule specific times to do their filming and training sessions.
LightingLighting in a virtual training environment can make the difference between an enjoyable, effortless viewing experience or a frustrating viewing experience. Poor lighting conditions might complicate a client’s view and make it difficult to comprehend exercises and instructions. The fitness professional should assess the lighting in their training space prior to filming and make all necessary adjustments until they are certain the viewer has no trouble seeing the instructor in the frame. Lighting in a building can vary greatly. Some rooms are lit through natural light by way of windows, some from skylights, and some from various artificial lights at different angles.

If natural light beaming through windows or skylights is the primary light source of the space, the fitness professional will need to evaluate the times of day they will be filming or performing training sessions. Filming exercise videos or performing training sessions as the sun sets may provide a poor experience for the client. 
Dmytro Zinkevych/Shutterstock.comThe time of day the filming will be performed (e.g., sunrise, midday, or sunset) will also need to be considered. Ideally, the fitness professional should pick times when it is brightest in the room to be filming or administering training sessions. Filming a practice video or performing a mock training session can help the fitness professional determine if natural lighting is adequate for their needs. When evaluating lighting, the main goal is to ensure that the fitness professional and the exercises being performed can be properly viewed and are undistorted to the camera. If the lighting is such that it casts a shadow on the fitness professional or over-illuminates them, adjustments will need to be made to the camera position or the time of day the filming is performed, or artificial lighting equipment may be needed.

### Exercise Area

Chapter 6Implementing Coaching SessionsLesson 2Training EnvironmentExercise Areafaboi/Shutterstock.comThe style of flooring in a virtual training environment will affect the exercise and footwear selection for the fitness professional. Flooring surfaces can vary from hardwood and tile to carpet and rugs. If the training space contains slick flooring, such as tile, linoleum, or hardwood, the fitness professional will need to make sure they wear appropriate footwear during their sessions. Conducting sessions in shoes with worn soles or while in socks is ill-advised on slick surfaces as accidents and movement complications may be more likely to occur. If the space contains rugs or has a carpeted surface, the fitness professional may need to put less thought into footwear. Some longer carpets like shag-style carpet can be slick. Slick carpeting may cause trouble gaining traction and increase injury risk while in footwear with worn soles or insufficient ankle support. The fitness professional should test the movements being performed beforehand and determine footwear requirements. 
Another aspect of flooring that warrants consideration is visibility. If the flooring is distracting or complicates the client's ability to see the instructor’s extremities or equipment placement, adjustments will need to be made. These might include adjustments to the exercise selection or flooring type to provide an optimal viewing experience for the client. Additional flooring adjustments that can be made to optimize the client’s experience include laying down solid-colored, low-profile rugs, using foam or rubberized mats, and coaching on flooring void of shag-style carpeting and potentially distracting graphics or patterns. Adjustments to exercise selection are dependent on the flooring available. If the flooring is hard (e.g., hardwood, tile, or linoleum) the viewer is not likely to have trouble seeing the placement of extremities (e.g. hand placement or foot placement), whereas, thick rugs and shag-style carpeting may complicate a viewer’s understanding of proper placement of extremities (e.g., foot placement in a squatting exercise) and exercise selection will need to be adjusted.
Once the flooring in the space has been assessed and adjusted for functionality, the fitness professional will need to ensure that their training equipment will not affect or be affected by the flooring material. Heavier training equipment such as dumbbells, kettlebells, or any weighted object will not distort the viewer’s experience, but they may have an impact on the flooring in the space. Hardwood, linoleum, and tile surfaces can be damaged and cause the camera to shake if a piece of equipment is dropped. If the flooring surface is at risk of damage or rattles the camera when it hits the floor, the fitness professional should place a suitable floor covering such as a rug or rubber mat over the area where training sessions will occur. Rubber training mats and thick rugs are sufficient for absorbing vibration and protecting flooring from damage.

If the fitness professional is choosing exercises that involve equipment such as foam rollers, exercise sliders, or an exercise ball, they must test the flooring surface to ensure the exercise movement is not restricted. For example, utilizing an exercise ball on a carpeted area may increase the risk of the ball sliding out.
Environment Safety ConsiderationsFitness professionals should also inform clients about best practices regarding flooring safety when exercising. Slick flooring, such as hardwood, tile, and linoleum floors, can be dangerous surfaces to perform lateral, jumping, or explosive movements on, especially in improper footwear. If the client is wearing footwear with minimal traction (for example, socks), slick floors are appropriate for performing gliding or sliding movements such as a mountain climber exercise. However, if the client were to attempt a plyometric or explosive lateral movement in just their socks, there would be a higher risk for the client slipping and getting injured. The fitness professional should educate their clients on appropriate footwear and adjust the exercise selections when their flooring poses a potential injury risk.

Additionally, the way in which professionals present themselves, whether it be online or in-person, speaks volumes about their competence and professionalism. Presenting to clients in a clean, clutter-free, and organized space goes a long way in demonstrating competence. This also displays a courtesy to clients and conveys that the professional takes their business and the client’s time seriously.

Virtual training may require relocation to a new space, whether it be due to traveling or renting a new training space. Regardless of where a fitness professional is conducting their training sessions, they should make their best effort to keep the space as clean and organized as possible. This is not to suggest that remodeling and reconstruction are necessary. The primary objective of the fitness professional should be to do their best to keep the space they operate in as orderly and clean as possible. ### Equipment Access for the Fitness Professional

Chapter 6Implementing Coaching SessionsLesson 2Training EnvironmentEquipment Access for the Fitness ProfessionalMaridav/Shutterstock.comEquipment access is a primary limitation of virtual when compared to in-person training. When a fitness professional works at a gym, they may have unlimited access to workout equipment, whereas, when at home or running a smaller virtual coaching business, equipment access may be limited. When considering workout programming, the exercises chosen for the client are more dependent on what type of equipment the client has versus what equipment the trainer has on hand. This is because the trainer can still demonstrate many movements without specifically having those machines or extra equipment available. This is also where filming exercise videos to use as demonstrations when writing client programming can help minimize the equipment needed when performing the virtual coaching sessions themselves. Ideally, the fitness professional should have some basic equipment available to demonstrate movements and show proper form. Dumbbells or resistance bands generally suffice; the fitness professional does not need a full gym worth of equipment to administer a quality training session.

### Client Equipment Purchasing

Chapter 6Implementing Coaching SessionsLesson 2Training EnvironmentClient Equipment PurchasingClient equipment access will be the primary consideration when selecting appropriate exercises. Programming should consider what equipment the client has available so that the fitness professional is prepared with an appropriate exercise program design. The client and fitness professional do not need to have matching equipment. For instance, the client may use a dumbbell for a goblet squat, while the trainer uses a kettlebell. Small changeups like this mid-session will be easily managed. Client equipment requirements may also be dependent on goals. If a client is trying to build as much strength as possible, or maximize muscle growth, working out at home with a set of 5-pound dumbbells and resistance bands may not provide the adequate loading required for those goals. If the client is looking to move better and feel better and does not have specific performance goals, a small selection of bands or dumbbells will suffice.
The most efficient way to determine what equipment will be needed for a training session is for the fitness professional to ask their client what their goals are, where the client will be training (at home or at a gym), and what equipment they will have access to. It is up to the fitness professional to educate the client on equipment requirements. ### Client Instruction and Virtual Communication Strategies

Chapter 6Implementing Coaching SessionsLesson 3Client Instruction and Virtual Communication StrategiesClient Instruction and Virtual Communication StrategiesEffective communication is an important aspect of a successful virtual training session. Clients engaging in a virtual training program will be relying on the communication and instructional skills of the fitness professional conducting their session. Fitness professionals need to be able to effectively instruct, coach, and motivate clients with little more than their voice and movement. Virtual clients will be able to see and hear their instructor performing exercises, but effective training sessions also require detailed coaching, demonstration, and preparation. The ability to prepare for, communicate with, and instruct clients whom the professional may never be in the same room with is what sets great virtual fitness professionals apart from mediocre ones.
Workout and Session StructureWhen designing a training program for a client, outlining and organizing the training sessions creates a road map the fitness professional will use to help their clients get to their destination. Equipment access, training frequency, training duration, and missed appointments are all aspects that will alter the structure of a training program from client to client. The objective is to get the client from Point A to Point B in the safest, most effective, and efficient manner possible. Many obstacles may arise along the way, but as mentioned previously, designing a plan prior to starting will help to mitigate complications during the journey. This may mean the fitness professional must adapt quickly to a busy client's changing needs.
The organization of workouts may be dictated by several factors such as equipment availability, training intensity, and training frequency. Whatever the case may be, the fitness professional should organize workouts based on the needs of their clients and utilize a system to keep track of which workouts will be scheduled and when. Organizing and keeping track of workouts will aid in keeping clients on track to obtain their goals and increase training program efficacy.

### Visual and Verbal Instruction

Chapter 6Implementing Coaching SessionsLesson 3Client Instruction and Virtual Communication StrategiesVisual and Verbal InstructionThe most vital aspect of virtual training is effective instruction. Without the luxury of in-person communication, where a trainer and client can clearly see and hear each other, it is imperative for the fitness professional to execute clear and concise visual and verbal instruction to their clients.

As mentioned earlier in this chapter, sound checks and camera positioning will need to be tested and adjusted to ensure all demonstrations are visible and instructions audible for the chosen recording device. Anything in the space that is obstructing or distorting the view or audio of the instruction will need to be removed or adjusted. Once a thorough sound and camera check have been completed, the fitness professional can then focus on the delivery of their instructions.

Verbal instructions should be clear and easy to understand. The fitness professional should speak at an appropriate volume and enunciate as they speak.
Drazen Zigic/Shutterstock.comIt is also best to limit fitness jargon and complex instructions that could potentially confuse a client. For example, while describing a squatting exercise, the fitness professional would clearly and concisely verbalize the most important aspects of the exercise and avoid describing the exercise in complex or confusing language.

Visual instructions should be provided using proper form, a demonstrative pace, and by performing multiple repetitions of the exercise being shown. For example, while demonstrating a push-up for a client, the instructor will slowly perform multiple repetitions using proper form. By speaking clearly in a language and tone the client can easily interpret and by demonstrating in such a way that the client can easily determine what they should be doing, the fitness professional will be providing their clients with the best opportunity to understand the instruction.
Coach's CornerExample verbal Instructions for a squat exercise:

Clear and Concise: “Squat down like you are sitting into a chair and put your weight through the center of your feet as you stand back up.”

Complex and Confusing: “Squat down until your hips reach parallel while maintaining thoracic extension. Once parallel has been reached, press through your heels, squeeze your glutes, and bring yourself into hip extension.”
Try ThisThe fitness professional may consider the following example of instruction

“If you watch my elbows, they aren’t flaring out and making me shaped like a T, but, rather, they are tucked back slightly as if I’m trying to hold a tennis ball in my armpit.”

### Pre-, Intra-, and Post-Session Instruction

Chapter 6Implementing Coaching SessionsLesson 3Client Instruction and Virtual Communication StrategiesPre-, Intra-, and Post-Session InstructionBefore conducting a training session, the fitness professional should determine which style of instruction is best suited for the specific workout and/or their specific style of training. There are a few ways to effectively instruct a virtual training session: pre-, intra-, and post-session instruction.
Pre-Session InstructionPre-session instruction is the act of demonstrating or describing the components of an individual workout session or exercise routine prior to beginning the session. This may include demonstrating each individual exercise in the routine, going over all important details, or describing duration, intervals, and intensity levels. Pre-session instruction is best used for fast-paced, high-intensity style training routines. During fast-paced routines, there is not much time available for detailed instruction during the workout itself. By providing instruction or demonstration prior to this type of workout, the instructor allows the client to perform the routine without interruption and allows the fitness professional to have the opportunity to coach, motivate, and make minor corrections.
Helpful HintPre-session instruction is best suited for the following activities: 


    Pre-recorded workouts
    Group fitness settings 
    Circuits
    HIIT training
    Time-based intervals

### Intra- and Post-Session Instruction

Chapter 6Implementing Coaching SessionsLesson 3Client Instruction and Virtual Communication StrategiesIntra- and Post-Session InstructionIntra- and/or post-session instruction is a form of instruction that consists of a demonstration or correction of training variables during and/or after a training session or exercise bout. This form of instruction is useful when time or exercise complexity is not a limiting factor. This form of instruction may be most effective when a client is not performing a complex routine or has plenty of time to perform the reps and sets of their session.
Helpful HintIntra- and/or post-session instruction are best suited for the following activities:


    Low-intensity workouts 
    Low-complexity workouts
    Familiar workouts 
    Long-duration sets and workouts

If a client is performing an exercise for a long and continuous duration, for example, a 15-rep set of goblet squats, there is plenty of time for the fitness professional to coach the client and make form corrections along the way. A brief pre-session instruction could be utilized in conjunction with intra-/post-session instruction, but taking time to provide pre-session instruction could potentially take away from the time spent engaging in the actual session in a continuous, long-duration routine.
Coach's CornerThe following is an example of intra-/post-session instructions.

A client is scheduled to perform a simple exercise in which technique is not a major concern such as goblet squats. The client is going to do the exercise for three sets of 12 to 15 reps. Goblet squats are a simple exercise that may not require much coaching or form correction. Rather than taking time away from the session by demonstrating and explaining the exercise in detail, the fitness professional can have the client perform the squats. If any necessary corrections are needed, the fitness professional understands there will be plenty of time to correct them during those sets without impacting the training session.

### Cueing Exercise Techniques

Chapter 6Implementing Coaching SessionsLesson 3Client Instruction and Virtual Communication StrategiesCueing Exercise TechniquesAn exercise cue is a condensed or simplified version of instructions used to help clients more easily understand or achieve a training or exercise objective. Providing exercise cues to a client during a training session is an effective coaching tactic. Cues can be as simple as instructing a client to drive their hips back or as creative as telling them to try to spread the floor with their feet. In the virtual training environment, because efficiency is of the essence, cues serve as a fantastic coaching tool. Simply instructing a client to squat may not be a sufficient form of instruction and going into great detail about each aspect of an exercise may cause frustration and confusion for clients.
Exercise cues bridge the gap between understanding and implementation. A training client will need to understand the primary components of an exercise in a time-efficient and easy-to-comprehend manner. The fitness professional will not need an exhaustive list of cues for every exercise, but it is beneficial to develop some cues for the exercises that are utilized in programs. These cues can also be used to help clients gauge exercise or training difficulty. Providing a cue or scale for intensity to help guide and quantify client training intensity can serve as a more effective method than using more complex instructions such as “74% of 1RM” (Sweet et al., 2004).

A rating of perceived exertion (RPE) scale is a number-based scale used to gauge the level of exertion or difficulty of an act or event (Figure: RPE Scale). An RPE scale can be used by a fitness professional to easily prescribe an intensity level to perform an exercise at and by a client to comprehend and communicate difficulty levels.

### Exercise Selection

Chapter 6Implementing Coaching SessionsLesson 3Client Instruction and Virtual Communication StrategiesExercise SelectionProper exercise selection is not exclusive to virtual training, but it warrants consideration due to potential equipment barriers. Preparing exercise programs and selecting exercises based on a client’s equipment and space allows for quicker, more efficient adjustments to be made when needed. If the fitness professional does not know what equipment their client will have access to, they should incorporate movement patterns that could easily be performed both weighted or unweighted, with only minor alterations. By choosing exercises in this fashion, the fitness professional will avoid having to make drastic training program changes in real time if their client lacks the necessary equipment. Variables such as sets, reps, tempo, and rest periods can always be adjusted to increase training difficulty when an exercise that is intended to be performed with external resistance now has to be performed with just bodyweight.
DC Studio/Shutterstock.comFood for ThoughtHere are some considerations for adjusting training difficulty and intensity:


    Repetitions: Goal dependence – Increase the number of repetitions in each set.
    Sets: Increase the total number of sets being performed of a specific movement.
    Antagonistic Supersets: Complete sets of antagonistic exercises back to back with minimal to no rest in between sets. For example, performing a lat-pull down followed by a chest fly. The pull-down is a pulling exercise and the chest fly is a pushing exercise.
    Agonistic Supersets (Compound Sets): Performing similar movements for the same muscle group(s) back to back to create additional stimulus or stress – For example, performing a chest fly and then doing push-ups right after
    Tempo or Time Under Tension: Increase time under tension or increase the tempo of sets and repetitions.     Rest Periods: Decrease rest time between sets or include an active rest period in which the client is instructed to perform a low-intensity activity during prescribed rest times.

The tone of the fitness professional should be uplifting, motivational, and function-focused. A 2018 study found that function-focused coaching had more positive effects on client body-satisfaction than appearance-focused coaching (Engeln et al., 2018). ### Physical Readiness and Injury History

Chapter 6Implementing Coaching SessionsLesson 4Conducting Small or Large Group TrainingPhysical Readiness and Injury HistoryFitness professionals should never attempt to diagnose or treat any form of illness; however, they should develop an understanding of the physical readiness of their clients. By providing clients with readiness questionnaires and reviewing their injury history, the fitness professional will have a better understanding of any limitations a client may have. Developing programs and awareness around a client’s physical readiness will help the fitness professional maintain a safe virtual training program and environment for their clients.

Previous injuries, health conditions, lifestyle habits, and work environment all provide vital information used for shaping individual programs. However, if a client presents with a serious medical or health condition, the fitness professional should refer them to an appropriate medical professional for clearance before participating in training or physical activity.
Critical!Fitness professionals, who are not licensed medical professionals, should never attempt to diagnose, treat or prescribe for any form of disease, illness, or medical condition.
Equipment SafetyEquipment safety is a byproduct of proper fitness professional demonstration and exercise selection. Fitness professionals should demonstrate exercises that require equipment and request that their clients demonstrate competency before progressing with more complexity, intensity, or increased weight. This demonstration can be completed through video calls or through form videos sent via e-mail or direct message. Demonstration of competency is needed, especially when a client has never used a particular piece of equipment or done a specific exercise movement.

Aligning exercise selection with client needs and capabilities mitigates equipment-related injury risk. If selected exercises are safe and the client has demonstrated competency, progressing the client to more challenging movements, incorporating added equipment, increasing difficulty, or adding more resistance to the movements can be done safely. If the client has not shown proficiency in performing an exercise safely, keeping the client at a regressed version of the exercise may increase exercise safety overall and minimize the potential risk of equipment-related injuries.

### Nutrition Program Development and Delivery

Chapter 6Implementing Coaching SessionsLesson 4Conducting Small or Large Group TrainingNutrition Program Development and DeliveryNutrition program delivery is much like delivering virtual training sessions. They will also need to choose how to communicate with and manage clients. There are a variety of tools the fitness professional or nutrition coach can utilize to develop nutrition plans for clients. There is an array of client management systems and the fitness professional should do their research to consider which option will work best for both the client and coach. Professionals can choose to utilize one or a combination of all these tools as long as they are able to effectively produce, provide, and discuss nutrition plans with their clients.

Chapter 6Implementing Coaching SessionsLesson 4Conducting Small or Large Group TrainingSummaryThe fitness professional or nutrition coach sets the stage for the client’s virtual experience. The environment they construct, the way they present themselves, and the coaching they provide all dictate the experience the client will have in their virtual space. A successful virtual fitness professional understands and applies the principles of environment, presentation, and effective communication to implement great training sessions and provide excellent nutrition coaching that can rival in-person coaching.

By using the guidelines mentioned in this chapter, the fitness professional will be able to effectively adjust and configure a virtual training environment, schedule and organize training and coaching sessions, determine appropriate training equipment, and instruct a variety of clients virtually.
