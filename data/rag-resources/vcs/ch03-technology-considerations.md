### Computer Processing

Chapter 3Technology Considerations Lesson 1Hardware RequirementsComputer Processing The first puzzle piece of a computer’s hardware requirements is computer processing. When choosing a computer, it is important to ensure the processing capability aligns with the intended use of the machine. A personal home computer used for basic computing, some streaming, and Internet access will have significantly different processing needs than a computer intended for small business use. In order to make the best purchase for building a virtual coaching business, the fitness professional should understand what components and requirements are needed to operate effectively, efficiently, and productively.
The central processing unit, or CPU, is the brain of a computer (Figure: CPU Chip). It carries out and directs all tasks being performed. The faster a CPU is, the faster and more efficiently the computer will run. A fitness professional may be using Excel spreadsheets for exercise programming and have a video being uploaded to their workout directory for clients, while also using instant communication applications on their computer—all of which can consume processing power.
Weera.otp/Shutterstock.comFigureCPU ChipHaving enough processing capacity to keep a computer running at optimal pace, while multiple programs are running, can ensure productive workflow.
CPU speed is currently measured in gigahertz (GHz), but, historically, when processing power was lower, they were in megahertz (MHz). A gigahertz is also known as “Clock Speed.” At 2.5 GHz, it is like a clock that ticks 2.5 billion times in a second. Each tick is an opportunity for the CPU to move and modify the smallest fragments of data called bits. The higher the GHz classification for a processor, the quicker a processor can process data and make decisions. Modern computers typically range from 2.0 GHz through to 5 GHz or more as technology advances. While top-of-the-line processor speeds are not needed for virtual coaching, the fitness professional should ensure the machine being purchased has between 2.4 and 4.0 GHz of processing speed.
Getting TechnicalAnother component of CPU technology to consider is how many cores the processor has. Each core can work on a single task. The more cores a processor has, the more tasks a CPU can process. An average computer may come with a dual core processor but can contain more. Ideally, aiming to purchase a machine with four cores or more will help with multitasking and running multiple programs without slowing the computer down.

### RAM

Chapter 3Technology Considerations Lesson 1Hardware RequirementsRAMRAM stands for Random Access Memory and is another piece of the puzzle that makes up a computer system (Figure: RAM). RAM is essentially a computer's short-term memory.
daniiD/Shutterstock.comFigureRAMRAM temporarily stores the information that the computer needs to access immediately or in the near future.

RAM keeps track of everything that is being processed by the computer such as open web browser tabs, a training spreadsheet being edited, or a video being rendered. Instead of the computer going into a hard drive storage device to pull the data it requires, RAM knows what the computer will need next and keeps that information at hand, ready to be used.
For example, RAM is like having a grocery shopping list in hand at the store. Having this list shortens the shopping trip; without it, the shopper would walk aimlessly up and down the aisle trying to remember what is needed for groceries and extend the time spent shopping!
Getting TechnicalRAM is short-term memory, whereas a hard drive is used for long-term storage. In fact, RAM does not actually store anything. RAM is what is considered volatile memory, meaning it loses its data as soon as the power is shut off. The data and information are only housed temporarily, as needed. Hard drives are considered non-volatile memory, meaning the data is stored even when the power is turned off.
RAMRandom Access Memory – A computer's short-term memory for processing immediate and upcoming tasks.

### How Much RAM is Needed?

Chapter 3Technology Considerations Lesson 1Hardware RequirementsHow Much RAM is Needed? RAM is currently measured in Gigabytes (GB). How many GB of RAM a computer has will determine how much data it can temporarily store for instant access as the computer is operating. The amount of RAM needed will depend mainly on what the computer is being used for and the complexity of the program. The more complicated a program becomes the more RAM will be required. A simple program, such as a basic notepad, will not take up much RAM, if any. Complex programs like Excel, Photoshop, or a video-editing software may use gigabytes of RAM by themselves.
With virtual coaching requiring programs like Excel spreadsheets, video editing software, or video conferencing programs that can consume a large amount of RAM by themselves, it is a good idea to ensure you have enough RAM rather than not enough (Table: RAM Recommendations). This can also minimize expensive RAM add-on kits or having to purchase a new computer if there is no option to expand RAM capabilities. It saves money in the long-term and minimizes frustrations with a slow operating computer system.

Coach’s CornerIn most cases, 8 to 16 GB of RAM will be enough to effectively and efficiently run a virtual coaching business. When purchasing a laptop or desktop, ensuring it has a minimum of 8 GB of RAM, is a great starting point. 16 GB of RAM would be enough for the majority of the process and software requirements for running a virtual coaching business.
Food for ThoughtMore RAM is not always better. While it is a good idea to get more than needed, rather than not enough, getting 32 GB of RAM when the max operating requirements are less than 16 GB may be wasteful. It costs more money and does not make the computer faster unless the software programs actually need 16 GB+ of RAM.

### Local Storage

Chapter 3Technology Considerations Lesson 1Hardware RequirementsLocal StorageLocal storage has the benefit of instant access and is typically faster than uploading and downloading information from the cloud. Plus, if an Internet connection goes offline temporarily, a virtual coaching business can still operate because of the data being accessible on demand, as needed. Local storage devices such as thumb drives, the physical storage device on the computer, or an external drive (Figure: Local Storage) are also more secure than cloud storage because information can only be physically accessed with those storage devices in hand. However, if those drives become lost, stolen, or crash, there is a risk of data loss.
There are two main types of hard drives to consider for local storage:


    Hard Disk Drive or HDD: A physical disk that spins inside a case where the data is written and erased. They are typically cheaper but also larger and slower than other types of hard drives.
    Solid-State Drive or SSD: A solid-state storage device is similar to a thumb drive. It has no moving parts and is faster than a standard HDD but also comes at a higher cost.

Anton Marchenkov/Shutterstock.comFigureLocal StorageFood for ThoughtConsider getting an external hard drive that is kept at home, which regularly backs up all the vital information for running a virtual coaching business. If a computer or phone is lost, or something gets damaged, that external drive will safely store all that data and it is easily accessible.

### Cloud Storage

Chapter 3Technology Considerations Lesson 1Hardware RequirementsCloud StorageChristian Horz/Shutterstock.comCloud storage has the advantage of being accessed on the go with most cloud services being accessible from a phone or laptop. This means a virtual coaching business can be easily conducted from a smartphone or laptop and a Wi-Fi signal. However, there may be higher security risks with cloud-based storage. Because the data is stored in a third-party system, there may be some added risk of attackers hacking into the system and gaining access to the data (Tujilio et al., 2016). Alongside added security risks, large file uploads and downloads may be more time consuming than transferring between a computer and a physical external hard drive.

When picking a cloud storage service, be mindful of scaling a virtual coaching business. Dropbox and Google Drive both come with word processing and spreadsheet capabilities and a large amount of initial free storage. Google Drive has some added benefits such as integrated e-mail and business services and other professional software such as Google Sheets (spreadsheets), Google Docs (word processing software), and the ability to create forms for collecting client information and sign ups.

Both local storage and cloud storage have their pros and cons, and using a mixed approach of both cloud storage and local storage will be most effective for managing and running a virtual coaching business.

### Video Card

Chapter 3Technology Considerations Lesson 1Hardware RequirementsVideo CardThe final piece, a video card, or graphics card, is hardware that takes all the data from across a computer system and generates it into a visible image on the computer monitor or laptop screen (Figure: Video Card). Most video cards include a stand-alone processing unit, built-in memory that is similar to RAM, and sometimes a cooling fan to help manage temperatures. It is essentially another computer in itself. Just like RAM, a video card is designed to take added stress off the CPU to more efficiently process images and transfer them to the monitor or screen.
teh_z1b/Shutterstock.comFigureVideo CardMany graphics cards will also have additional outputs such as an HDMI connection or VGA connection. HDMI connections transmit both audio and video data, whereas a VGA connection only transmits video data. Some graphics cards will also include an S-video display and USB port, which can connect to digital cameras, webcams, and other peripherals like microphones.

For doing high-quality exercise videos, filming and editing social media content, or creating PowerPoint presentations, the built-in video card that comes with any mid-range desktop or laptop will meet the needs of a virtual coaching business. Upgrading video cards might be considered if the machine being purchased will be used for high-definition video editing, gaming, or other professional editing software uses.

### Desktop or Laptop?

Chapter 3Technology Considerations Lesson 1Hardware RequirementsDesktop or Laptop? A fitness professional should consider whether a desktop computer or laptop computer will be needed to build a virtual coaching business. There is more available processing power in a typical desktop computer than in a laptop computer when looking at a comparable price range. While a laptop has all of the hardware requirements built into it as a complete package, a desktop computer will have an added cost of the wires, monitor, speakers, mouse, and keyboard. Once these pieces are factored in, a high-quality laptop can be purchased for a similar price with more than enough processing capabilities for a virtual coaching business.

Bandwidth is the maximum data transfer rate of a network or Internet connection, in a specific amount of time, or how fast a person can download or upload information to and from the Internet. Bandwidth is measured in megabytes per second or Mbps. A Megabyte (Mb) is broken up into smaller units known as Kilobytes (kbit) and there are 1,000 Kilobytes in each Megabyte of data. Kilobytes are broken up into bits and there are 1000 bits per kilobyte. There is also a Gigabyte, which is 1,000 megabytes (Mb).

### Lighting Spectrum

Chapter 3Technology Considerations Lesson 3Computer Peripheral RequirementsLighting Spectrum Lighting spectrum or color temperature is what hue of color is being provided by a light source. Degrees of Kelvin (K) is how the appearance of light is measured. Kelvin is measured from 1,000 K up to 10,000 K. The lower end of the Kelvin spectrum has a far-red tinge to it. The high end of the Kelvin spectrum has a blue overtone.
The typical lighting spectrums used in video content is between 3,200 K and 5,500 K. The 3,200 K lighting spectrum is typically associated with indoor lighting and indoor scenes; it will be warmer in color with a higher degree of orange or yellow. The 5,500 K spectrum is a cooler light that is mostly bluish-white and is representative of midday daylight. Too low of a lighting spectrum will produce more flat colors and may create unnatural skin tone, while too high of a lighting spectrum will lack warmth and be harsh to look at.
The 5,600 K spectrum may be more optimal for use where there is a lower level of light or in indoor settings where incandescent bulbs or soft, warm lighting may be used. The midday lighting spectrum will help produce a brighter, more natural and balanced feel. The warmer 3,200 K spectrum may be more optimal for use where there is harsh white light such as under fluorescent bulbs or when filming in brightly lit locations. The warmer spectrum may reduce the harsh look that higher spectrum lighting may produce.
Many of the available lighting options will have adjustability from 3,200 K to 5,600 K, which means the fitness professional will be able to adjust the lighting spectrum accordingly for where they will be filming the majority of their content. Having this adjustability can minimize purchasing extra equipment or lighting options.

### Audio

Chapter 3Technology Considerations Lesson 3Computer Peripheral RequirementsAudioWhen filming or recording high-quality content for a virtual coaching business, fitness professionals should ensure that the sound quality of the content being produced is considered. Poor audio quality or videos with excessive background noise may lead to the client retaining less of the information and impair learning and communication (Klatte et al., 2013). This may lead to added struggles and create a discord between the client and the fitness professional. Purchasing a microphone can help create a quality audio feed when recording content such as podcasts, training videos, and voice-overs, and when performing virtual coaching sessions.



There are two main types of microphones to consider:


    Dynamic Microphones
    Condenser Microphones


Dynamic microphones are the types of microphones one might see being used by a singer or comedian performing on stage (Figure: Dynamic Microphone). These microphones tend to do better than condenser microphones in environments that have a higher level of background noises. That is why dynamic microphones are used in concerts, during stand-up shows, or interviewing somebody on the street. They contain a coil and magnet inside that are sensitive to sound vibrations. Dynamic microphones are also more rugged than condenser microphones, so, if the microphone gets dropped, there may be less worry of damage and having to replace an expensive peripheral. These microphones are relatively cheap compared to condenser microphones and require no extra equipment. They can sit at a desk in the quiet to be used for podcast recordings or used in a noisy environment such as filming workout videos.
Soonios Pro/Stuttershock.comFigureDynamic MicrophoneKrakenimages.com/Shutterstock.comFigureCondenser MicrophoneCondenser microphones work slightly differently than their dynamic counterpart (Figure: Condenser Microphone). Condenser microphones do not contain a coil and magnet and instead use capacitor plates, which are more sensitive to the smaller sound vibrations than a dynamic microphone. This can help pick up the subtle differences in a voice and may be better for recording a podcast in a quiet area. Condenser microphones are also not as durable as a dynamic microphone due to their construction using capacitor plates. Condenser microphones may also need an external power source or internal battery to operate. Depending on the type of microphone purchased may mean hidden equipment costs such as extra audio equipment. However, condenser microphones that are powered by battery or USB plug-in are becoming more available making these microphones a viable option for fitness professionals without added costs.

### Wearables

Chapter 3Technology Considerations Lesson 4Fitness Programming Software OptionsWearables Wearable fitness technology has been around as early as 1981, when Olympic medalists were using wearable heart rate monitors. Commercial gyms started seeing wireless heart rate monitors in the early 2000s being integrated into group workout classes. Since 2010, when Fitbit released the first clip-on pedometer, technology has gone from basic step counting to tracking heart rate, sleep, energy expenditure, and even GPS tracking.
Clients can track virtually every meaningful data point about themselves, which can be helpful but can also provide additional confusion in an already overwhelming abundance of information. Keep in mind, the current body of evidence is mixed regarding the weight-loss and health benefits from wearable fitness technology (Jo et al., 2019). While the direct benefits for the client may still be in question from a research perspective, there are valuable metrics that can be tracked such as heart rate, sleep, and energy expenditure. The fitness professional can use this information to help customize and evaluate exercise programs. When considering what is best for a client, note that some of the metrics being tracked by wearable technology will be more accurate than others.
Heart Rate wavebreakmedia/Shutterstock.comThere are multiple studies showing that heart rate monitoring will be most accurate at rest or at moderate activity levels, with accuracy decreasing as exercise intensity increases (Khushhal et al., 2017). If a client is doing higher-intensity or long-duration cardiovascular training such as cycling or running, a better option to recommend is a fitness tracker that comes with a wearable heart rate chest strap (Reddy et al., 2018). If a client is looking for general heart rate monitoring, a wristband style fitness tracker will suffice.
Energy Expenditure Many fitness trackers will estimate total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) and provide the end user with a calorie burn count at the end of each day. How accurate is this measure of TDEE? Studies show that most wearable fitness trackers have a range of accuracy and, much like heart rate, as exercise intensity increases, measured energy expenditure accuracy may decrease (Kendall et al., 2019). When compared to research-grade fitness trackers, resting calorie expenditure may be close. However, these devices tend to overestimate time spent in higher-intensity activities, which leads to higher-than-actual energy expenditures being reported by the wearables (Feehan et al., 2018). This simply means that current wearables may do well at calculating energy expenditure at rest, but the level of accuracy decreases as exercise intensity increases. One study showed a 20 to 50% difference in actual calories expended versus what the wearables listed when recording energy expenditure during resistance training (Bai et al., 2016). However, it is important to note that the quality of the device’s electronics and algorithms have a fundamental influence on the accuracy of the device. In other words, some wearables are more accurate than others. Due to the high degree of inconsistency across wearable fitness trackers, fitness professionals should use caution when relying on this information.
Energy expenditureThe number of calories burned over a specified period of time at a given intensity of movement.
Total daily energy expenditure (TDEE)The number of calories a person burns over a 24-hr period.

### Step Counters/Pedometers

Chapter 3Technology Considerations Lesson 4Fitness Programming Software OptionsStep Counters/Pedometers Most step counters are fairly accurate when it comes to measuring stride length and accurately representing step counts, with an average error of 3 to 6% on some brands (Feehan et al., 2018). There are also multiple studies that show an improvement in all-cause mortality that is associated with higher step counts (Dwyer et al., 2015). In addition to the health benefits of a higher step count, monitoring step counts can help a fitness professional monitor for a subconscious decrease in Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis (NEAT) that is often associated with weight-loss-based goals among clients (Trexler et al., 2014).
Kaspars Grinvalds/Shutterstock.comNEAT includes all movements and activities of daily living that are not structured exercise such as fidgeting, performing household chores, grooming, and pacing. If a client starts out at a 10,000-per-day step count average at the start of a weight-loss program and through their program ends up subconsciously reducing step counts to 7,000 per day, there may be a notable difference in TDEE. By monitoring step counts, clients can be encouraged to move more and avoid this downward metabolic adaptation that occurs while working to achieve a fat-loss-related goal. This is where wearable fitness technology excels in terms of accuracy, alongside being the most usable data for the fitness professional.
Coach’s CornerThe fitness professional should always try to meet the client where they are at when it comes to step counts. Assigning a 10,000-per-day step count to a client who is only achieving 2,000 steps per day may feel overwhelming to the client. Try to encourage the client to move more where they can while helping them understand NEAT can impact daily energy expenditure. Set small goals, even just 5 to 10 minutes of extra movement a day may contribute greatly to overall step counts over a week.
SleepOne very popular function of many fitness trackers that has evolved over time is the ability to monitor sleep. Sleep impacts appetite (Brondel et al., 2010) and hunger and can affect the client's rate of recovery from their training sessions. Most fitness trackers have sleep monitors that are shown to be relatively accurate when compared to self-reported sleep time (Lee et al., 2018).
Andrey_Popov/Shutterstock.comWhile the sleep trackers on wearable fitness technology may not be on par with the technology used to monitor sleep in clinical research studies, for the client and the fitness professional, the accuracy of sleep tracking in wearable fitness technology will suffice in assessing sleep hygiene. Monitoring a client's sleeping patterns may help the client to improve sleep hygiene and overall sleep quality.
Food for Thought6 hours and 40 minutes is the average amount of sleep an American gets (Sleep Foundation, 2014).
Non-exercise activity thermogenesis (NEAT)The measurement of energy expenditure from all other movements outside of intentional exercise activity.

### Integrating Apps and Wearables Into Business

Chapter 3Technology Considerations Lesson 4Fitness Programming Software OptionsIntegrating Apps and Wearables Into Business Whether using the NASM Edge App, third-party client management software, or any of the other apps listed, it is important for the fitness professional to understand how to best use these integrations when operating a business. Remember that apps and software programs have their advantages and disadvantages and there is no one correct approach for building a virtual coaching business. Budget, business scaling, client population, and ease of client use all play a role in determining which programs and apps to use. For example, when getting started, a fitness professional may choose to simply use spreadsheets for workouts and nutrition tracking. This simple system can be a low-cost solution for the fitness professional and can help their clients build effective health habits. Over time, integrating wearables such as a pedometer or activity tracker can help the client more accurately track their physical activity and will add additional value.
If a fitness professional is working with a higher client load or scaling a business and building a team of coaches, using an all-in-one client management software program may lead to a more scalable and productive approach. All-in-one client management software systems may incorporate the functions a fitness professional may need:


    Billing and invoicing systems
    Client reminder and check-in systems
    Brand customization
    Nutrition tracking
    Syncing with wearable fitness to track activity
    Workout videos and exercise programming tools

Even if the fitness professional is not operating with a high volume of clients, many of the benefits that client management software systems offer will help a small-scale online business run smoothly and productively.
The software and app options are major considerations for creating the foundations of client management and a professionally delivered coaching experience in a virtual coaching business. Each client management software system has their own unique features; therefore, it is important to conduct further research and consider all aspects that will be needed for operating a virtual coaching business.
While these options and recommendations have the fitness professional in mind, it is also important to consider the client when using these different approaches. The less-complicated the system being used, the lower the barrier of effort it will be for the client. This lower barrier of effort for coaching interaction means a client can focus on the coaching itself rather than learning a complicated system. For some, filling out spreadsheets may be an effortless portion of their check-ins, but for others it may be a barrier of effort that causes additional struggles in adherence to the coaching program. Using a simple system such as a client management software program may allow the fitness professional to reduce time spent performing other tasks and give their clients a higher level of service. It also may reduce these critical barriers of effort for the client.
